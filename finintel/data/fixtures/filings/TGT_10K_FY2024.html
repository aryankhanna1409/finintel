PART I

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our results depend on discretionary consumer demand. A significant share of our sales mix is in discretionary categories such as home, apparel and hardlines, and continued pressure on consumer budgets from elevated prices and interest rates reduced traffic and comparable sales during the year. Prolonged softness in discretionary spending would adversely affect our sales, margin mix and earnings.

Changes in trade policy, including tariffs on imported merchandise, could increase our costs. A meaningful portion of our owned-brand and national-brand assortment is sourced from outside the United States. New or increased tariffs may raise product costs, and we may be unable to fully offset them through vendor negotiations, sourcing changes or pricing without harming demand.

Our brand and reputation may be harmed by public reaction to our positions, merchandise decisions or business practices. Customer and advocacy-group responses, including calls for boycotts and negative social media attention, have in the past and could in the future reduce guest traffic and sales.

Inventory shrink, including theft and organized retail crime, has affected and may continue to affect our gross margin. While shrink moderated from the prior year, elevated levels would pressure profitability.

We face intense competition from multi-category retailers, warehouse clubs, dollar stores and digital marketplaces, many of which compete aggressively on price, assortment and delivery speed. Competitors with greater scale may be able to absorb cost increases more effectively.

A failure to manage inventory levels and assortment, including excess discretionary inventory, could require markdowns that lower our gross margin rate.

We rely on information systems and could be subject to a significant cybersecurity incident that compromises guest or team member data.

Our ability to execute our strategy depends on attracting and retaining key leaders. A change in senior leadership could disrupt execution of strategic initiatives.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Comparable sales were approximately flat for the year as growth in digitally originated sales and same-day services offset softer store traffic in discretionary categories. Gross margin rate expanded on lower markdowns and moderating shrink, partially offset by higher digital fulfillment and supply chain costs.

Operating income margin rate was 5.2 percent. We returned cash to shareholders through dividends of approximately $2.0 billion and share repurchases of approximately $1.0 billion, and we remained committed to maintaining our middle-A credit ratings.
